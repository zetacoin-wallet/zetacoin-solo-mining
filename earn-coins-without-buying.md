There are two legitimate ways to earn ZET without buying it: staking coins you already hold, which pays 0.0625 ZET per Proof-of-Stake block, and mining with Scrypt hardware, which pays about 0.1 ZET per Proof-of-Work block. Both are participation in block production. Anything promising ZET for tasks, clicks or airdrops is not describing this network.

## The two real mechanisms

Zetacoin's rewards come from consensus. Staking signs blocks with coins you hold; mining computes hashes with hardware you run. Both are paid by the protocol itself, not by a company, and both are proportional to what you contribute.

That framing rules out most of what people hope for. There is no faucet, no referral programme and no bounty scheme run by the project. A reward that arrives without either coins at stake or hardware hashing did not come from the chain.

| Method | What you need | Reward | Realistic expectation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Staking | mature coins, running wallet | 0.0625 ZET per PoS block | small, irregular |
| Mining, pool | Scrypt hardware, pool account | share of 0.1 ZET blocks | small, regular |
| Mining, solo | Scrypt hardware, synced node | full 0.1 ZET blocks | rare |

The full walk-through, with the checks in order, is in [How to Stake Zetacoin](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-wallet-guides/blob/main/how-to-stake-coins-in-the-client.md).

## Staking, if you already hold ZET

Staking is the cheapest route because it needs no purchase beyond the coins themselves. The wallet must be synced, unlocked for staking only, and left running; coins mature for about eight hours before they can be selected.

The reward is a flat amount per block, so a larger balance earns more blocks rather than bigger ones. For a small balance, that means long gaps between stake entries, which is normal rather than a fault.

## Mining, if you have Scrypt hardware

Mining requires Scrypt equipment, because the chain has used Scrypt since the 2021 rebuild and SHA-256 hardware cannot mine it. A pool converts hashrate into regular small credits; solo mining through your own node keeps whole block rewards but arrives rarely.

Power cost is the real variable. On a chain with a 0.1 ZET block reward, hobby mining is participation rather than income.

For the background behind this step, [How to Mine Zetacoin on Scrypt](https://github.com/zetacoin-wallet/zetacoin-solo-mining/blob/main/how-to-mine-on-scrypt.md) covers the same ground in more detail.

## What to be suspicious of

Anything that asks you to send coins in order to receive more, sign a message to "claim", or share a wallet file is a theft attempt. The chain has no claim mechanism, and there is no airdrop from this project that requires a transaction from you.

"Cloud mining" offers deserve particular caution: they ask for payment up front and produce nothing you can verify on the chain. If you cannot point at a block that paid you, you are not mining.

## What about holding and receiving?

Receiving ZET is not earning, but it is the other way coins arrive without a purchase. If someone pays you in ZET, the wallet receives it and, once mature, it can stake.

That makes a small payment flow self-sustaining in a modest way: coins received can earn, and earned coins add to the balance that earns later.

## Costs people forget

Staking costs the electricity of a running machine, which for an always-on node is real but small. Mining costs the electricity of the hardware plus its purchase price. Both cost the opportunity of leaving a balance accessible rather than locked in a staking state.

None of those is prohibitive, and none of them is zero. The honest comparison is against what the same resources would earn elsewhere, which is a personal calculation.

## Common questions

### Can I earn ZET without any coins or hardware?

Not from the protocol. Rewards come from staking or mining, and both require a contribution.

### Are there airdrops?

The project does not run one. Offers that require a transaction or a wallet connection are not from the project.

### Is cloud mining real?

Treat it as unverifiable unless you can point to blocks on the chain that paid your address.

### Does staking require a minimum?

No. There is no minimum balance; small balances simply produce blocks less often.

## The short version

Stake what you hold, mine if you have Scrypt hardware, and ignore everything that asks for coins or keys to give you more.

## Where the rewards come from, and why that matters

A reward that comes from consensus is paid by the protocol and can be checked on the chain. That is the property separating staking and mining from every offer that appears in a message or an advertisement.

When a wallet produces a Proof-of-Stake block, the payment is visible as a stake entry in its own history. When a miner finds a block, the payment is visible as a coinbase transaction to the address that was configured. In both cases the ledger records the payment, and in both cases the amount matches the protocol's rules rather than a platform's promise.

That check is available to anyone: point at the block that paid you. An offer that cannot produce that evidence is not a way to earn ZET, whatever it calls itself.

## What a small balance realistically does

A balance that produces a handful of blocks a month is earning roughly what the protocol says it should. Expecting a stake entry every day from a modest balance is expecting the network to select the same wallet more often than its weight justifies.

The useful mindset is that earnings are a slow accumulation measured over months. A wallet left running, with mature coins and staking enabled, will produce entries; a wallet that is watched hourly will look inactive most of the time simply because most hours contain no block for it.

Related operational pages: {{ARTICLE:zetacoin-staking-rewards-explained}} explains the amount per block, and {{ARTICLE:zetacoin-wallet-resource-usage}} covers what running the node costs.

## How to tell an earned reward from a promised one

An earned reward leaves a trail on the chain: a block, a coinbase output or a stake entry, and a matching change in your balance. That trail is what makes the two mechanisms trustworthy and what makes every other kind of offer distinguishable from them.

A promised reward leaves a trail of requests instead. Send coins to activate. Connect a wallet to claim. Share a file to verify. Each step asks you to give something up before anything is returned, which is the opposite order from a protocol payment.

The habit that follows is simple: before participating in anything that offers ZET, ask which block will pay you and which address it will pay. If that question has no answer, there is no mechanism.
